# The administration at a glance

An entry for the administration appears behind the gear as soon as your role carries at least one
administration right. That also holds for a custom role with a single such right. Whoever has
every administration right sees Admin there, whoever may only read numbers sees Statistics,
everyone else sees Administration.

## The areas

The sidebar only shows what your role has a right for. A page without a right exists for you
neither in the sidebar nor by address. Opening it lands you on Jobs, or on the first page you may
see without that right.

- **Services**: releasing the ten services and the jobs of every person
- **AI Assistant**: how materials are read in, plus the language trainer, the language test, and
  the AI's behaviour per language level
- **Limits**: how much material comes in and how much of it goes into the model, shared by
  services and the AI assistant
- **Statistics**: numbers on users, services, the AI assistant, models, and the evaluation.
  Nothing is set here
- **Providers & models**: the AI providers with address and key, and which function uses which
  model
- **Users**: who gets in and who may do what, plus impersonations, feedback, and the evaluation
- **System**: maintenance mode, the start page notice, and the welcome window
- **Mascot**: Hilde and Francis in every pose, meant for development only

## Who sees what

Every page hangs on its own right in the Manage chapter of the rights matrix. That lets you build
a role that, say, only reads the feedback. How roles and rights connect is in the article Users
and rights.

## When something takes effect

Settings save as soon as you leave a field or flip a switch, and apply immediately for everyone.
Only the evaluation, a person's quotas, and dialogs such as for a new provider carry a Save
button. There is no preview and no test mode.

## What the installation brings

Whether an installation has the AI assistant, the services, or both is set by the server. A
missing part also means its pages and rights are missing. Without services there is, for example,
neither release, nor jobs, nor limits.
